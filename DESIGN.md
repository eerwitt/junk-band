# JUNK visual system

The web build always plays full motion. It does not read the operating system's reduced-motion preference and has no motion setting; the reduced-motion behaviour described below remains in the engine but is not reachable from the browser.

Paper-cutout starter artwork is explicitly generated; uploaded media remains untouched. Silhouette shadows and deterministic beat-derived poses give the stage physical presence. Pickup preserves the material grip and captured orientation while a snappy spring and slight gravity let the mostly rigid cardboard trail the pointer. Tilt, sway and deformation stay bounded. Drops settle at their committed destination without snapping to old scene data. Stationary drops and arrivals carry real weight: the falling cutout stretches slightly, slams into a held compression of up to about 10% within 40 ms, kicks up into one small hop, takes a lighter second hit at 310 ms and rocks out the remaining energy. Beneath it, a pressure wave embossed through the paper is lit from the stage's sun direction and leaves a dark dent with hairline creases that erodes over 1.25 seconds. A skirt of 15–24 noise-eroded dust puffs is blown flat along the floor before rising, split in front of and behind the actor by depth, with a column of displaced air behind the body. Tumbling cardboard chips bounce twice, and eleven hand-inked impact lines snap shut within 200 ms. The stage takes a brief ringing kick, and neighbours within 210 world units hop as the wave reaches them. Fixed viewport controls do not move. A quiet plop and a readable hand-lettered impact word (15–17 pixels), stamped on with a paper knockout, last 0.72 seconds. Reduced motion removes ambient and impact spatial effects while retaining feedback and a quiet held depth pose: a stationary wave mark, a light dent and the word. The circle and sparse typography must survive phone layout.

A released performer slides when its floor gap exceeds eighteen world units or its speed exceeds 260 world units per second; a gentler set-down is a full stationary drop. Downward velocity brings it to contact within about 55 ms, then a slower critically damped floor spring (omega 10.5) continues toward the committed destination without a landing rebound. Landing energy is shared between floor and travel: a faint impact wave, dent, dust skirt and chips scale down from 260 world units per second to nothing at 900, and only stationary drops get inked impact lines. The body takes a brief touchdown compression, then crouches and leans back against the skid, up to 0.27 radians, then pitches past upright through an underdamped spring when it brakes below 85 world units per second. Musical elevation is suppressed only while the grounded slide is settling; note articulation continues, and normal note bounce resumes afterward. A continuous dry-brush ribbon with twin heel tracks, foreshortened to the floor plane, traces the actual floor route from release, including a lightly pressed precontact portion, until speed falls below one world unit per second and the destination gap falls below 0.2 world units. Its head stays under the feet between samples. The stop endpoint is retained. Sampling uses two-screen-pixel spacing and at most forty-eight samples, coarsening long routes while preserving both ends. The slide is deliberately quiet: the mark is a faint narrow dusting, touchdown throws a small fan of dust puffs back, and while the feet are moving a timed wake throws distinct puffs up and away behind them, with loose dirt specks that arc and bounce, so even a short slide reads as particles, and faint ink speed lines appear only above 650 world units per second. Particle records are capped at 64 puffs, 48 chips and 14 lines. Cleanup starts 500 ms before the slide's measured stop (never sooner than 150 ms after contact), because the floor spring creeps long after the slide visibly ends. The trail then slowly erodes from release toward the stop, oldest marks first. The sweep is spaced by distance along the route and takes 0.7 to 1.4 seconds depending on its length, with a 700 ms local fade for each mark. Reduced motion retains the full stationary ground trail and the same opacity sequence without expanding particles. Each drop emits one landing event and audio acknowledgement. Impact words keep their own 0.72-second fade and sit outside the projected body bounds, including the remaining slide sweep, on the side opposite horizontal travel. Their baseline is twenty-four screen pixels below the bounds, with only two pixels of downward drift in normal motion. Words follow floor-contact geometry independently of the cursor, keeping the performer clear.

Deliberate additions and clones enter with a short rigid release from 25.5 world units above their committed floor position. Actual gravity, the existing silhouette-to-footprint depth model and one collision drive the entrance; contact reuses the compression/rebound, dust, ink flecks, ripple, impact word and quiet plop. Imported performers stay hidden and cannot intercept pointer input until their actual poster is ready; a new rigged arrival also waits for every body/limb layer. A missing imported texture never substitutes starter artwork. The arrival event is transient, so hydration, project opening and Undo do not replay it. Picking up during the fall cancels its pending arrival collision, and a recorded path remains free to move. Reduced motion keeps one floor acknowledgement without height, fall or deformation. Existing landing-effect concurrency limits bound simultaneous entrances.

Add is a large sheet floating over the washed-out stage, sized so a laptop screen needs no scrolling, while the band fades to near silence over about a second and fades back on close. Choosing the object is four stamped paper steps: pick the frame on grey film where only the frame in use shows in colour, paint a rough mask with the brush (the only selection tool), say what it is or leave it for a guess, and watch the scissors go round what you painted. The as-is look is only the selected object, with everything else in the frame gone to white, on a torn scrap of white paper, so an unstyled picture still belongs among the cutouts. The pencil records a freehand route and the eraser clears it. Smooth cubic interpolation follows the drawing at distance-based timing, then returns to the stored home position over the last two bars with zero seam velocity. A drawn path is a closed musical gesture, with no teleport at the next loop.

Pickup feels snappy and mostly rigid, with a spring damping of 18 and frequency of 20. Faster pulls introduce at most 1.8% stretch, 1.2% shear and a small lean, with limited recoil when the hand reverses or stops. Pickup first loads toward the floor over 90 ms with a 1.2% rigid compression, then a separate spring raises the cutout toward a thirty-world-unit carry height. There is no idle sine sway. Regrabbing an airborne cutout preserves its visual grip without replaying the grounded loading phase. Reduced motion retains a gentler follow and a fixed twelve-world-unit held height, without the loading dip, deformation, oscillation or gravity. Its shadow mask shrinks and loses shear over 100 ms toward the 0.4 height ratio, without switching to the diffuse ellipse at this modest height; release returns directly to the floor. Reduced-motion arrivals remain at zero height without a fall.
